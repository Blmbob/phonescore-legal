Buying a used iPhone: what to check

The full checklist, in order: what to check before you go, what to test on the spot, and what to require before paying. Fifteen minutes that save you from losing hundreds.

The short answer

Before: check the IMEI (blacklist, iCloud lock, carrier lock). On the spot: matching IMEI, your SIM card, battery, screen, Face ID. Before paying: the seller signs out of their Apple Account and erases the iPhone in front of you.

1. Before meeting

  • Ask for the IMEI, or a photo of the *#06# screen. A seller who refuses doesn’t need any more of your time.
  • Run a check: blacklist, iCloud lock, carrier lock, real model, warranty. A single red flag is enough to cancel.
  • Compare with the listing: model, capacity, color. A 128 GB sold as 256 GB shows up here.
  • Place the price: well below market, there is almost always a reason.

2. On the spot: identity

  • A public place, in daytime, ideally near Wi-Fi and with your own phone charged.
  • *#06#: the IMEI shown must be the one you checked, and the one on the box.
  • Settings: no name at the top, otherwise an Apple Account is still signed in. In General → About, “Carrier Lock” should read “No SIM restrictions”.
  • Your SIM card: insert it and make a call. This is the test that catches a carrier lock or a blacklist.

3. On the spot: condition

  • Battery: Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging. Below 80%, factor a replacement into the price.
  • Parts: on recent models, Settings → General → About shows parts and service history and flags an unknown or non-genuine part (screen, battery, camera).
  • Screen: a white then black background to spot marks and dead pixels, and touch across the whole surface.
  • Face ID or Touch ID: set up a face or fingerprint. They are often lost after a repair outside Apple’s network.
  • Cameras, mic, speakers, buttons, charging port: a photo, a video, a voice memo, a speakerphone call.

4. Before paying

  • Sign-out of the Apple Account in front of you: Settings → name → Sign Out, with the seller’s password.
  • Erase: Settings → General → Transfer or Reset → Erase All Content and Settings. The iPhone should restart on “Hello” and activate without asking for someone else’s account.
  • Receipt: seller’s name, date, IMEI, price. A receipt for an outright purchase also protects against a block for unpaid bills.
  • A traceable payment rather than cash, and keep the listing and messages.

What to do: if even one of these steps is refused, walk away. An honest seller loses nothing by accepting them.

What about a refurbished iPhone?

Bought from a professional refurbisher, an iPhone is normally tested and sold with a warranty: the safest option if the price suits you. Still check the IMEI: a refurbished phone can be put back on sale with an iCloud lock or a blacklist.

Not to be confused with the “refurbished by Apple” status a report may show: it means Apple restored the device to new. It isn’t a defect, but it justifies a price below new.
