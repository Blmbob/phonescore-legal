iCloud lock check before you buy

An iPhone or Mac that is still iCloud locked is only worth its spare parts. Here is how to spot the lock, have it removed in front of you, and why no “unlock” sold online works.

The short answer

Open Settings: no name should appear at the top. Otherwise, the seller must sign out of their Apple Account in front of you, with their password. If they can’t or won’t, don’t buy: only the owner can remove the lock.

What is the iCloud lock?

Its real name is Activation Lock. It turns on by itself when Find My is on, and ties the device to its owner’s Apple Account. After an erase, the device asks for that account’s Apple ID and password before it can be used.

It protects iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and recent Macs (Apple silicon or T2 chip). It is what makes a stolen device useless, and why so many are sold “iCloud locked”.

Signs the lock is on

  • A name at the top of Settings (iPhone, iPad) or in System Settings (Mac): an Apple Account is signed in, and Find My is probably on.
  • The “Activation Lock” or “iPhone Locked to Owner” screen at startup, asking for an Apple ID.
  • A message from the owner on the lock screen: the device has been marked as lost in Find My.
  • A seller who “forgot the password”, or promises to remove it later.

Check on an iPhone or iPad

Open Settings. If a name appears at the top, ask the seller to tap it, then Sign Out: their Apple password is required, and signing out turns off Find My.

The safest way is to go all the way to an erase: Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Erase All Content and Settings. The device restarts on the “Hello” screen. If it then sets up without asking for someone else’s Apple Account, the lock is really gone.

What to do: insist these steps are done in front of you, before paying. An honest seller needs two minutes.

Check on a Mac

Hold the Option (⌥) key, open the Apple menu and choose System Information. Under Hardware, the “Activation Lock Status” line reads Enabled or Disabled.

If it reads Enabled, the seller must sign out of their Apple Account in System Settings, then you can check again.

What to do: a Mac sold erased and switched off proves nothing. Ask to see it start up, and to read that line.

Who can remove the lock?

The owner, at any time: on the device, or remotely from icloud.com/find, by removing the device from their account. That is the way out if you bought a locked device from a seller who is reachable and acting in good faith.

Apple, only with the device’s original proof of purchase. A receipt from a reseller or a private seller is not enough.

Nobody else. “iCloud unlock” services sold online don’t remove the lock: at best they bypass the activation screen for partial access, with no calls and no Apple Account, which breaks at the next update.

Check the iCloud lock remotely
