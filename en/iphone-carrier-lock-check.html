iPhone carrier lock check

An iPhone locked to a carrier only accepts that carrier’s SIM cards, often a foreign one. Here is how to spot it in thirty seconds in Settings, and what can really be done to unlock it.

The short answer

Open Settings → General → About and look for the “Carrier Lock” line: it should read “No SIM restrictions”. Then insert your own SIM card and make a call. If either fails, the iPhone is locked.

What is a carrier-locked iPhone?

Some carriers sell the iPhone cheaper in exchange for a contract, and lock it to their network: it rejects any other SIM card. This is called SIM lock, network lock or carrier lock.

It is common on imported iPhones, especially from the United States, Japan or the United Kingdom. A locked phone is neither stolen nor faulty: it simply can’t be used outside its original carrier.

How to check

  • In Settings: General → About → “Carrier Lock”. “No SIM restrictions” means unlocked. Anything else means a lock.
  • With your SIM card: insert it, wait for signal and make a call. A “SIM Not Supported” or “Invalid SIM” message settles it.
  • Before meeting: an IMEI check shows the carrier lock and the purchase country, which saves a wasted trip.

What to do: don’t settle for an iPhone that “has signal” with the seller’s SIM. The test that counts is with your card, on your carrier.

US iPhones: eSIM only

Since the iPhone 14, models sold in the United States have no physical SIM tray. Even unlocked, they only work with an eSIM. If your carrier doesn’t offer eSIM, the phone will never get signal, whatever its condition.

What to do: look at the side of the phone. No SIM tray? Make sure your carrier provides eSIM before buying.

Can it be unlocked?

Yes, by the original carrier, and only by them. They usually agree once the contract has ended and the plan is paid off, sometimes at the account holder’s request. Apple cannot unlock an iPhone on the carrier’s behalf.

Online services sell unlocks: some really go through the carrier, many don’t work. Chips that slide under the SIM card to bypass the lock are unreliable and often stop working after an iOS update.

What to do: a locked iPhone isn’t worth the price of an unlocked one. Only buy on a promise of unlocking if the price accounts for the risk that it fails.
