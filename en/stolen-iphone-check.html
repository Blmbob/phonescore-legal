How to check if an iPhone is stolen

Before buying a used iPhone, three checks are enough to know whether it has been reported lost or stolen. Here are the warning signs, and the checks that settle it.

The short answer

Get the IMEI by dialing *#06#, then check two things: that the IMEI is not blacklisted, and that iCloud Activation Lock is off. If either one is on, don’t buy it, whatever the price.

Warning signs

None of these proves anything on its own. Two together mean you should check everything before paying.

  • A price well below the market. A recent iPhone selling for 30% less than everywhere else almost always has a reason.
  • A seller in a hurry. Meeting on the street, refusing to let you handle the phone, pushing to close fast.
  • No box and no receipt, or a receipt in someone else’s name.
  • A lock screen with someone else’s name, an “iPhone Locked to Owner” message, or an Apple ID prompt at startup.
  • An IMEI that doesn’t match between the phone, the box and the listing.
  • A cheap “brand new, still sealed” iPhone. Shrink wrap is easy to redo: it proves neither origin nor condition.

Step 1 — Get the IMEI

The IMEI is the phone’s unique 15-digit identity number. It is what carriers report when a phone is stolen.

Dial *#06# on the phone’s keypad and it shows up right away. You can also find it in Settings → General → About, and on the box label.

What to do: compare the IMEI on screen, in Settings and on the box. If they differ, the box belongs to another phone, and maybe the listing too.

Step 2 — Check the blacklist

When an iPhone is reported lost or stolen, the carrier adds its IMEI to a blacklist shared between carriers in many countries through the GSMA database. A blacklisted phone can be cut off from the network at any time.

It still works on Wi-Fi, so a seller can show it to you “working” without quite lying. The blacklist also covers phones blocked for unpaid bills: not necessarily stolen, but just as unusable.

What to do: if the IMEI is blacklisted, don’t buy it. Knowingly reselling a phone reported stolen puts you personally at risk.

Step 3 — Check iCloud Activation Lock

Activation Lock, tied to Find My, links the iPhone to its owner’s Apple Account. After a reset, it asks for their Apple ID and password again. A thief cannot remove it, which is why stolen iPhones are often sold “iCloud locked”, or with a promise of unlocking.

In 2017 Apple removed its public page for checking this lock from an IMEI. Two options remain: a checking service, or a test in front of the seller.

What to do: open Settings: no name should appear at the top. Otherwise, ask the seller to sign out of their Apple Account in front of you. If they refuse or stall, walk away. No “iCloud unlock” sold online can be trusted.

Already bought a stolen iPhone?

Don’t resell it: you would pass on the problem, and the risk with it. Contact the seller again and keep every record of the deal (listing, messages, payment).

If the phone is reported stolen, go to the police: that is also what protects you if its origin is held against you. Beware of “unlocking” offers: on an iCloud-locked or blacklisted iPhone, they don’t work.

What a check doesn’t tell you. It covers the device’s identity and status, not its condition: battery, screen, water damage. And a phone can be reported stolen after your check. A clean report is a green light, not a guarantee: always inspect the device.
