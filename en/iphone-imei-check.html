iPhone IMEI check

The IMEI is the phone’s ID card. Read properly, it tells you whether the iPhone is the one being sold to you, and whether it is reported stolen, iCloud locked or carrier locked. Here is where to find it and what it reveals.

The short answer

Dial *#06# on the iPhone and the IMEI appears. Compare it with the one in Settings and on the box, then run a check. It confirms the real model and flags blacklist, iCloud lock and carrier lock.

What is an IMEI?

A 15-digit number, unique worldwide, given to every phone when it is made. Carriers use it to identify the device on their network, and to block it if it is reported stolen.

iPhones that support two lines (SIM and eSIM) have a second number, the IMEI2. The serial number is Apple’s own identifier: it is the one used for a MacBook, which has no IMEI.

Where to find an iPhone’s IMEI

  • Dial *#06# on the phone keypad: the fastest way, and the most reliable since it comes from the device itself.
  • Settings → General → About: scroll down to “IMEI”. Touch and hold to copy it.
  • On the box, under the barcode on the label.
  • On the SIM tray of some models, engraved in very small print.
  • Locked iPhone or Activation Lock screen: on the Hello or Activation Lock screen, tap the ⓘ button to show the IMEI and serial number.

What to do: read the IMEI in at least two places. If they differ, the box or the listing belongs to another phone. It is often the first sign of a scam.

What an IMEI check reveals

From the IMEI, a full check queries several databases and answers the questions that decide a purchase:

  • The real model — including capacity and color, whatever the listing says.
  • The blacklist — has the IMEI been reported stolen, lost or unpaid to a carrier?
  • iCloud lock — is the device still tied to its owner’s Apple Account?
  • Carrier lock — does it only accept SIM cards from one specific carrier?
  • Warranty and history — purchase date, remaining Apple coverage, refurbished, replaced or demo device.

Free or full check?

Many websites offer a free IMEI check. It is usually limited to the device model: useful to spot a fake listing, not enough to know whether it is stolen.

iCloud status in particular can no longer be looked up publicly since Apple removed its checking page in 2017. Blacklist and carrier lock data come from paid databases. That is why full checks are paid too.

For warranty alone, Apple offers its own free page, from the serial number: checkcoverage.apple.com.

What the IMEI doesn’t tell you. It covers the device’s identity and status, not its condition: battery, replaced screen and water damage don’t show up. The databases queried can also lag behind. A clean report is a green light, not a guarantee: always inspect the device.
