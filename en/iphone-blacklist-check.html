iPhone blacklist check before buying

A blacklisted iPhone turns on, connects to Wi-Fi, and will never make a call. Here is what the blacklist means, why a phone ends up on it, including for unpaid bills, and how to check.

The short answer

Check the IMEI (*#06#) before buying, then insert your own SIM card and make a call. A blacklisted iPhone shows “No Service” with a valid SIM. Only the carrier that listed it can remove it.

What is the blacklist?

When a device is reported, the carrier adds its IMEI to a block list. Many carriers share it with each other and across countries through the GSMA database, the global association of mobile operators.

A listed device is refused on the mobile network: no calls, no texts, no data. It still works on Wi-Fi, so it can be shown “in perfect condition” with nothing looking wrong. Depending on the country and carrier, the block is national or shared abroad.

Why an iPhone gets blacklisted

  • Theft or loss — the owner reported it to their carrier, often along with a police report.
  • Unpaid bills — the iPhone was bought on credit or with a plan, and the payments stopped. The phone isn’t necessarily stolen, but it is blocked exactly as if it were.
  • Fraud — a contract taken out under a false identity, or a phone reported stolen to claim insurance and then resold.

Blacklisted for unpaid bills: the delayed trap

This is the trickiest case. A seller buys an iPhone on monthly payments, resells it right away, then stops paying. On the day of the sale, the IMEI is clean: every check is green. Weeks later, the carrier blacklists it, and the buyer is left with a silent phone.

Only the account holder can have the block lifted, by paying what they owe. The buyer has no recourse with the carrier.

What to do: be wary of a very recent iPhone sold without a receipt for an outright purchase. Ask for it: a phone paid in full cannot be blocked for unpaid bills. Also keep a record of the seller and the payment.

How to check

Before meeting: ask for the IMEI and run a check. It shows whether the device is already blacklisted.

On the spot: dial *#06# to confirm the IMEI, insert your SIM card and call someone. A seller who refuses this test, or only shows the phone on Wi-Fi, probably has a reason.

What to do: if the IMEI is blacklisted, don’t buy it. Knowingly reselling a phone reported stolen puts you personally at risk.

Can an iPhone be removed from the blacklist?

Yes, but only by the carrier that listed it, at the request of the line holder: phone found, debt paid, error corrected. Neither Apple, a repair shop nor a buyer can do it.

Services that promise to “clean” an IMEI for a fee are ineffective at best. Changing a phone’s IMEI is illegal in many countries.
